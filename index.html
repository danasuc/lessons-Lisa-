3. Reading Comprehension

Historical Venice (1733)

My New Home in Venice, 1733

Uncle Leo gives me a suspicious look when I call this place the 'Scacchi Palace'. It is really a house, although elsewhere in the world this would easily be regarded as a palace, even if it is in need of a little care and attention. Our house is by the side of the Rio San Cassian canal and a small square. We have a door which leads onto the street and two entrances from the water. One runs under a grand rounded arch into the ground floor of the house, which, as is customary in Venice, is used instead of a cellar for storing goods.

There is yet another exit: a wooden bridge with handrails runs from the first floor of the house straight over the canal and into the square itself. Consequently I can wander over it in the morning and find fresh water from the well in the centre of the square while still rubbing the sleep from my eyes. Or I can hail a gondola from my bedroom window! The house is almost 300 years old, built of dark brown bricks and with elegant arched windows. When I lie in bed at night, I hear the passing gondoliers singing in the square.

4. Grammar Section

Causative Have / Get

Theory: Causative have and get

Form: have / get + object + past participle (V3)

  • Meaning: We use this structure when we arrange for someone else (usually a professional) to do something for us.
  • She had the old garage turned into a study. (A builder did the work, not her).
  • I am getting my roof repaired tomorrow. (Roofers will repair it).
  • Adverse events: He had his bag stolen on the train. (Something unpleasant happened to him).
