3. Reading: The Colours of San Juan

Paragraph 1: Ancient Roots and Fire Rituals. Across the coastal towns of Spain, the arrival of midsummer is marked by the festival of Noche de San Juan. Although the celebration is officially linked with the feast day of Saint John the Baptist, its traditions originate in pre-Christian pagan ceremonies celebrating the summer solstice. Communities gather on local beaches as darkness falls, where gigantic bonfires are lit. According to centuries-old belief, the leaping flames possess purifying powers that burn away misfortune and prepare people for a prosperous harvest season.

Paragraph 2: Midnight Customs and the Sea. As the clock strikes midnight, the festivities reach their emotional peak. Spectators throw handwritten notes containing negative memories or bad habits directly into the roaring fires, symbolically cutting ties with their past. Immediately afterwards, hundreds of brave participants run into the cold sea waves. Ancient folklore dictates that bathing in the ocean at midnight cleanses the soul, cures ailments, and guarantees good luck for the rest of the year.

Paragraph 3: Visual Splendour and Modern Organisation. In modern times, San Juan has evolved into a vibrant public spectacle that blends heritage with contemporary music. While young people play guitars and dance around smaller embers on the sand, town halls organize dazzling fireworks displays and street parades featuring giant papier-mâché figures. Food stalls are erected along coastal boulevards, serving traditional coca bread topped with candied fruits and pine nuts.

Paragraph 4: Tourism and Ecological Concerns. Despite the undeniable cultural value of San Juan, the immense popularity of the festival has prompted heated environmental debates. Once the music fades and the crowds disperse at dawn, municipal cleaners are left with tons of discarded plastic bottles and charred debris scattered across the shoreline. As a result, local councils have recently introduced strict regulations, banning commercial wood pallets containing nails and urging visitors to keep the beaches clean.

4. Grammar: The Passive Voice in Festival Descriptions

Grammar in Culture: Form and Usage

When reporting public events, rituals, and traditions, the Passive Voice is preferred because the celebration itself is more important than who specifically carries it out:

Formula: Object + be (in the correct tense) + V3 (Past Participle)

• Present Simple: The festival is celebrated annually in June.
• Past Simple: Giant bonfires were lit along the coastline.
• Present Perfect: Strict safety regulations have been introduced recently.
• Modals: Cultural heritage must be protected by future generations.
